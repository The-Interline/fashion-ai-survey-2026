# Approved findings

Use only these public expressions. Do not restore exact figures, bases, cross-tabs, respondent descriptors or raw data.

## F01 — AI use is now routine

**Survey finding:** Around nine in ten respondents use AI in their personal lives. Around nine in ten use it day to day at work.

**Limitation:** This describes the surveyed cohort, not fashion professionals in general.

## F02 — Personal tools cross into work

**Survey finding:** Around two thirds bring the AI tools they use at home into work. Around one quarter do so without permission.

**Limitation:** This measures self-reported behaviour. It does not measure security incidents, policy compliance or tool quality.

## F03 — Use has outrun trust

**Survey finding:** Only around one quarter trust AI output enough to support critical decisions.

**Limitation:** The survey records confidence, not a tested measure of model accuracy.

## F04 — Adoption is accelerating

**Survey finding:** Around three quarters say their organisation has expanded its AI activity in the past year.

**Limitation:** More activity does not establish value, integration or organisational readiness.

## F05 — Integration remains shallow

**Survey finding:** Around two thirds describe AI initiatives as sandboxed or standalone tools with limited connections to other systems.

**Limitation:** The data records respondents’ descriptions of integration. It does not audit technical architecture.

## F06 — Measurement is weak while spend rises

**Survey finding:** Only around one quarter say their organisation has a good method for measuring AI return on investment. Around two thirds expect AI spending to increase. Expanding use or price rises within existing projects were cited more often than new projects.

**Limitation:** This is a forward-looking expectation and an editorially relevant spending pattern. It does not establish the future cost or value of specific tools.

## F07 — Failure has already entered the picture

**Survey finding:** Around two fifths have seen an AI initiative fail in the past year.

**Limitation:** A respondent’s observation of a failed initiative is not a project-level failure rate.

## F08 — The capability frontier maps to data availability

**Survey finding:** Respondents place AI’s perceived maturity in analysis and content, and its weakest areas in technical design, engineering, pattern development, sourcing and production.

**Limitation:** This is a perception of maturity, not a capability benchmark or vendor comparison.

## F09 — The workforce question is live

**Survey finding:** Around one quarter report AI-linked headcount reductions, hiring freezes or other cuts in their organisation. Around three quarters think AI could automate a third of their own role by 2030.

**Limitation:** These are reported organisational experiences and beliefs about future automation, not causal evidence of AI-driven job loss.

## F10 — Creative output will grow

**Survey finding:** Around three quarters expect the share of consumer-facing creative output created or touched by AI to increase within two years.

**Limitation:** This concerns expected use of AI-touched output, not a forecast of audience acceptance or commercial performance.

## F11 — The primary risk is bad judgement on bad information

**Survey finding:** Around three fifths identify critical decisions based on inaccurate, partial or hallucinated information as a potential negative impact.

**Limitation:** This is a stated concern, not evidence that a particular AI system will cause harm.

## F12 — Outlook is positive, but not settled

**Survey finding:** More than half are positive or optimistic about AI in fashion. Around one third remain uncertain.

**Limitation:** Sentiment is not a prediction of adoption, investment or commercial outcomes.
