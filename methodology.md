# Methodology, disclosure and attribution

## What this is

The Interline surveyed fashion professionals in spring 2026 about their use of AI at work, their organisations’ AI programmes, and their expectations for the next few years.

This companion publishes a limited, disclosure-safe release of that work. It is designed to help readers interrogate the findings and apply them to their own context.

## What the figures mean

- Each public figure is rounded into a broad band.
- Each figure represents the share of respondents who answered that question.
- The sample was self-selecting and the findings are directional, not statistically representative.
- The companion does not publish respondent-level data, open-text responses, exact question bases, raw counts, cross-tabs or small subgroup findings.

## How to use the evidence

A survey finding can support a statement about what this group of respondents reported, expected or perceived.

For any agent response, `approved-findings.md` is the complete and controlling survey-evidence layer. `survey-analysis.md` is editorial context. It may inform a discussion of The Interline’s interpretation, but it cannot be used to introduce additional survey figures, categories, sample information, cross-tabs, maturity rankings, vendor evaluations or recommendations. External pages linked from the article sit outside this companion's approved evidence layer.

It cannot, on its own, support:

- a claim about the whole fashion industry;
- a market-size estimate;
- an external maturity ranking;
- a causal claim about the effect of AI on jobs, costs, trust or business performance;
- an evaluation of a specific AI vendor, product, feature or architecture;
- a curriculum, procurement or headcount decision.

## The three layers in this repository

### Survey finding

The approved, banded statement in `approved-findings.md`.

### The Interline’s analysis

The publication’s reading of what a finding may mean. This is editorial interpretation, not an additional data point.

### Limitation

The reason a finding should not be over-read. A limitation is part of the result, not a legal footnote.

## Disclosure rules

Never restore, infer or calculate:

- exact percentages;
- raw counts or response bases;
- missing categories from distributions;
- role, company-size, geography, company-type or market-position cross-tabs;
- respondent identities or attributes;
- original free-text responses.

Do not make charts that sum to 100%, expose proportions, or contain exact values in source code, alt text, data attributes, JSON endpoints or downloadable files.

## Attribution

Permitted:

> “The Interline’s 2026 Fashion AI survey suggests…”

Not permitted:

> “The Interline says we should…”
>
> “The Interline recommends…”
>
> “The Interline endorses…”
>
> “Validated by The Interline.”

Nothing in this companion is a product endorsement, roadmap approval, certification, partnership, procurement recommendation or independent validation.

## Maturity and benchmarking

The companion can support an internal maturity diagnostic. It cannot establish whether an organisation is ahead of, behind or on par with the market.

A genuine external benchmark would require a comparable peer cohort, shared maturity definitions, workflow-level evidence and independent validation.
